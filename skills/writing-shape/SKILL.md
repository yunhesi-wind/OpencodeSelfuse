# Writing Shape

Shape raw material into articles through conversational exploration.

## Philosophy
Articles are not written — they are shaped. You have raw clay (notes, quotes, data, half-formed ideas). Your job is to find the shape hiding inside the material, then carve away everything that isn't the article.

## Process

### Step 1: Inventory
Read the raw material. List:
- Recurring themes (3-5 max)
- Strongest quote or data point
- Most surprising insight
- Weakest section that can be cut

### Step 2: Find the Shape
Ask the author these questions (one at a time, wait for answers):
1. "Who is this for? Describe your ideal reader in one sentence."
2. "What should they think/feel/do after reading?"
3. "If this article were a conversation at a bar, what's the ONE story you'd tell?"
4. "What's the most controversial or risky claim in here?"

### Step 3: Structure
Based on answers, propose a 3-5 section structure. Each section should:
- Have a clear purpose (what does the reader learn here?)
- Contain at least one concrete example or data point
- Build toward the conclusion

### Step 4: Draft
Write section by section. After each section:
- Show the draft
- Ask: "Does this capture what you meant?"
- Incorporate feedback before moving to next section

### Step 5: Polish
- Read the full draft aloud (mentally)
- Cut 10% of the word count — be ruthless
- Ensure the opening paragraph hooks the reader
- Ensure the ending doesn't just summarize — it should leave the reader with something

## Rules
- Never write ahead without author confirmation on direction
- One question at a time — don't overwhelm
- If author gives vague feedback ("make it better"), ask: "Better at what? Clarity? Persuasion? Length?"
